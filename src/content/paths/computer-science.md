---
title: Computer Science Fundamentals
tagline: The first year of a CS degree, from Harvard and MIT, for free.
audience: Anyone who wants to understand computing deeply — as a foundation for a career, further study, or serious self-directed work.
outcome: Working fluency in Python and C, a solid grasp of algorithms and data structures, and a self-built final project.
effort: 9–18 months part-time
order: 2
stages:
  - title: CS50 — the survey course
    description: Start with Harvard's CS50x. It is demanding, but it teaches how computers actually work — memory, data structures, SQL, the web — and its problem sets are the best free programming exercises anywhere.
    resources: [cs50x]
    checkpoint: Complete the problem sets and build the CS50 final project — your first substantial program, on a topic you choose.
  - title: Python properly
    description: Consolidate with MIT's 6.0001, the course MIT students take first. It cements computational thinking — decomposition, abstraction, algorithms — in Python, today's default language for science and data.
    resources: [mit-ocw-6-0001]
    checkpoint: Work the problem sets without looking at solutions. Publish your solutions to two of them on GitHub with notes on your approach.
  - title: Algorithms and data structures
    description: The material technical interviews and real engineering both stand on — sorting, hashing, trees, graphs, dynamic programming — from MIT's 6.006, with full lectures and problem sets.
    resources: [mit-ocw-6-006]
    checkpoint: Implement five classic data structures or algorithms from scratch in Python, each with tests, in a public repository.
  - title: Choose your depth
    description: A full CS education has nine subjects; nobody needs all of them at once. Use Teach Yourself CS to pick your next one — operating systems, networking, databases, distributed systems — and mine MIT OpenCourseWare for the course materials.
    resources: [teach-yourself-cs, mit-opencourseware]
    checkpoint: Pick one subject, finish one full course or book in it, and write a public summary of what you learned.
---
